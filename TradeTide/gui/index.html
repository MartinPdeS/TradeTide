GETTING STARTED

How TradeTide works

TradeTide runs historical FX backtests locally in your browser. Configure a market and strategy, simulate it against bundled data, then inspect and compare the completed experiments.

1

Choose market data

In Market data, select a currency against USD and a sample from 1 to 180 days. Presets provide common windows; all samples begin at the first bundled observation.

2

Build a strategy

In Strategy, enable and tune indicators, choose how their signals combine, and set position size, exits, commissions, and slippage. Save named configurations to reuse them later.

3

Run the simulation

Select Run backtest. The progress display follows real processing stages: loading data, computing indicators, combining signals, creating positions, simulating the portfolio, and calculating performance.

4

Learn from the run

Use Results for equity and indicator charts, Orders & trades for execution detail, and the comparison pages to assess variations.

Reading a backtest

Results
Shows full-run metrics, portfolio equity, market prices, and the values produced by each enabled indicator. Sliders inspect a portion of a chart without changing its metrics.
Orders & trades
Separates every simulated entry request from completed positions. A signal can be skipped when portfolio constraints prevent execution.
Compare
Select two completed runs to see their settings and performance side by side. Equity overlays require matching markets and observation times.
Research
Ranks the locally retained runs by return, Sharpe ratio, drawdown, or recency. Export a research report when you want to share the experiment set.

Important notes

  • Backtests use bundled historical data and simulated orders only; they never place live trades.
  • Drafts, saved strategies, and the 30 most recent completed runs stay in this browser. Export JSON or CSV before clearing browser data or moving to another computer.
  • A strong historical result does not guarantee future performance. Test meaningful variations and account for drawdown, costs, and trade count before drawing conclusions.
